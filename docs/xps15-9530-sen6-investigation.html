1. What Opus Initially Detected & What It Cost

While benchmarking Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (a 125B / 6B-active hybrid MoE running GSQ-RCO IQ3_S on the Strata 0.2.20 inference engine, 64k context, DFlash2 drafting), initial instrumentation by Opus 5.5 revealed a dramatic sawtooth collapse during sustained generation:

  • The symptom: Every ~2 minutes of continuous token generation, all 14 CPU cores abruptly locked to 400 MHz for 45–77 seconds, while the RTX 4070's PCIe link simultaneously dropped from PCIe Gen 4 (16 GT/s) to Gen 1 (2.5 GT/s).
  • The throughput cliff: Pre-brake decode ran smoothly at 27–31 t/s. During each stall, decode cratered to 7–10 t/s. Across a warm 6-round clean-boot baseline (strata-throttle-trace-20261124-1849-1-balanced.jsonl), the laptop spent 42.4% of all generation seconds (261 / 616 s) at 400 MHz and 256 s at PCIe Gen 1, dragging steady-state throughput down to 16.06 t/s (nearly half of peak speed).
  • Early elimination: Opus proved this was not a Strata regression (0.2.11 and 0.2.20 tied at 28.5–29.1 t/s unthrottled), not fixed by updating BIOS 1.13.0 → 1.34.0, and not CPU die overheating (one balanced trace logged 165 s at 400 MHz with just 18 ms of CPU package thermal throttling!).
  • The prime suspect: Every single brake in the first 22 observed episodes began shortly after an unnamed ACPI board sensor — SEN6 (read-only hot = 65.05 °C, hysteresis = 4.00 °C, critical = 70.05 °C) — hit 65.05 °C, and every completed episode released cleanly at 61.05 °C.
Initial Working Theory vs. What Needed Proof
The classic laptop lore for a 400 MHz lock is external BD PROCHOT asserted by the Embedded Controller (EC) over an overheating VRM. Before changing a single register or power limit, we committed to proving what SEN6 physically measures and which silicon mechanism actually forces 400 MHz.

2. How We Investigated & What We Actually Found

Using a strictly read-only diagnostic script (sen6-diag-readonly.sh), we dumped and decompiled the live ACPI tables (iasl -d), decoded Dell's compressed Intel DTT policy blob (INTC10A0:00/data_vault), and sampled Intel thermal/power MSRs, turbostat, and lspci -vv right across live brake transitions. Every classic assumption turned out to be wrong:

  1. ACPI SSDT4 (DptfTabl) identified SEN6 — and cleared the VRMs

    Decompiling SSDT4.aml exposed Dell's human-readable _STR strings for every EC sensor channel. SEN6 (EC channel 0x07) is "Charger Temperature Sensor(QE10)" — near the USB-C power-delivery / charger stage, not the CPU/GPU VRM. Meanwhile, the actual VRM sensor SEN9 ("VR_GPU&CPU(QE16)") peaked at just 75 °C against a 94 °C hot limit. The ACPI table also showed S7HP = 0x41 (65 °C) and S7CC = 0x46 (70 °C) are static AML constants returned to the OS and never written back to the EC — proving an initrd ACPI SSDT override could never move the EC cutoff.

  2. Live MSRs + turbostat ruled out BD PROCHOT completely

    Across 505 1 Hz samples spanning two full brakes (sen6-diag-brake.txt) plus a static snapshot after earlier brakes, every single PROCHOT active and sticky log bit (0x19C b2/b3, 0x1B1 b2/b3, 0x64F b0/b16) stayed 0 for the entire boot. Instead, bit 10 (0x0400 — Package/Platform Power Limit PL1) flipped 0 → 1 simultaneously across 0x19C, 0x1B1, and 0x64F in all 80/80 braked samples. Mid-brake turbostat logged Bzy_MHz = 400, UncMHz = 400, PkgWatt = 9.18 W, PKG_% = 101.89% (RAPL throttling counter), CoreThr = 0, and SMI = 0 — even while OS-visible MSR 0x610 and MMIO 0x59A0 still read 115 W. Conclusion: the 400 MHz lock is a hidden firmware package power limit (strongly inferred to be an EC PECI RAPL PL1 domain limit), not BD PROCHOT.

  3. PCIe Gen 4 → Gen 1 is firmware-commanded, not pcie_aspm=force

    lspci -vv on root port 00:01.0 (PEG010) and GPU 01:00.0 confirmed ASPM is unsupported/disabled on both ends. Mid-brake, 00:01.0's LnkCtl2: Target Link Speed itself dropped from 16GT/s to 2.5GT/s 1–2 s after the CPU hit 400 MHz, with zero NVML hardware slowdown or power-brake flags (0x08/0x40/0x80) asserted.

  4. Linux lacks Windows's Intel DTT policy — and Dell's _OSC handshake is one-way

    Decompressing the 2,956-byte INTC10A0:00/data_vault yielded 82,639 bytes (217 ESIF keys) of Dell's Windows DPTF table. On Windows, Intel DTT smoothly caps CPU PL1 on SKIN (46–55 °C → 45–20 W) and SEN5 (62 °C → 15 W) while setting SEN6's Windows trip points higher (_HOT = 70 °C, _CRT = 75 °C). Crucially, inspecting IETM._OSC in SSDT4 revealed that activating Intel DTT (or running Linux's stock thermald --adaptive) invokes ECDV.DPST(1), which writes 1 to EC register 0x32 (EC_DPTF_STATE_ENABLE) — and Dell's ACPI has no code path to ever write 0 back.

3. The Clean-Boot Plot Twist: EC Reg 0x32 & the Two-Stage Cliff

Our first custom thermald policy (SEN6 ≥ 63 °C → 22 W, without --adaptive) initially looked like a home run: 24.0–25.5 t/s steady, 0 s at 400 MHz, SEN6 peaking at 64.05 °C. However, peer review caught a critical flaw: that 5.5-minute run had occurred on the same boot after our earlier thermald --adaptive test had already latched EC reg 0x32 = 1.

We refused to trust or install anything until we performed a full cold shutdown (power completely off) and re-verified from a clean boot (kernel 6.18.63-3-MANJARO, NVIDIA 620.70.06, live SEN6 ≥ 68 °C emergency kill-switch guard):

  1. Cold shutdown restored the brake — and broke the 63 °C → 22 W policy

    A clean-boot unmitigated trace immediately reproduced 5/5 EC brakes (SEN6 65.05 → 61.05 °C, 16.06 t/s steady), proving cold shutdown clears EC reg 0x32. When we then ran the exact same 63 °C → 22 W XML on that clean boot, it hit 11 EC brakes in 15 minutes. Cross-referencing thermald's per-second RAPL package watt log revealed why: Intel's default RAPL PL1 averaging window is 55.97 seconds (constraint_0_time_window_us = 55967744). Waiting until 63.05 °C meant the CPU kept drawing 32–40 W for the entire 12–30 s until the EC brake slammed it — and on a clean boot, all 11 brakes fired at SEN6 = 64.05 °C.

  2. Moving the cap earlier caught Dell's 2-step EC fallback in slow motion

    We moved the cap early (59 °C → 20 W passive + 63 °C → 17 W max) so the 56 s RAPL window engaged well before 63 °C. That kept SEN6 ≤ 63.05 °C and held the CPU genuinely at 17.0 W — yet 9 EC brakes still fired! Because the transition happened slowly, the trace caught Dell's clean-boot EC fallback stepping in two stages:

    Across all 30 JSONL brakes recorded in this project, 30/30 began once SKIN reached 59.05–60.05 °C (with SEN6 = 62.05–66.05 °C), and 29/29 completed brakes released at exactly SEN6 = 61.05 °C.

  3. The thermal bottleneck: GPU watts heat SEN6 >2× harder than CPU watts

    Opus's regression across traces fit SEN6 ≈ 46.2 + 0.19 × CPU_W + 0.41 × GPU_W (R² = 0.87). With the RTX 4070 pulling a full 34.5 W, even cutting CPU down to 17 W (50.8 W total) still drove SKIN to 59.05 °C. Breaking free on a clean boot (EC reg 0x32 = 0) required trimming GPU heat slightly so total chassis power stays below ~48–49 W.

Why Option A3 Works & How to Use It Daily

Dropping GPU core clock alone hit the RTX 4070's voltage floor around 1000 MHz (28.7 W → 27.4 W), because the 7001 MHz GDDR6 array drew the bulk of the remaining power. Stepping VRAM down one P-state to 5501 MHz shaved another ~4–5 W off the hottest contributor to SEN6 and SKIN (dropping GPU draw to 24.4 W), giving enough thermal room to raise CPU PL1 to 24 W and run smoothly at 20.35 t/s steady (20.62 t/s overall across 8 rounds) with zero full EC brakes and zero PCIe Gen 1 drops.

Even better for an out-of-warranty laptop: total continuous draw drops from 68.2 W to 48.4 W (−29% heat load), thermald stays disabled, nothing is modified in /etc, and Dell's stock hardware protection remains 100% intact.

5. Proven Dead Ends (What Not to Do)

Save Yourself Time & Hardware Risk
  • Do NOT disable BD PROCHOT (wrmsr 0x1FC bit 0): Proven 0% effective here (PROCHOT never asserted across 505 live samples) and removes real emergency protection.
  • Do NOT patch SSDT4 _HOT via initrd ACPI override: S7HP = 0x41 is never sent back to the EC; changing it only changes what Linux prints.
  • Do NOT tear down the heatsink to re-pad VRMs expecting to fix SEN6: SEN9 (VR_GPU&CPU) runs at a comfortable 67–75 °C against a 94 °C hot limit.
  • Do NOT enable Manjaro's stock thermald.service (--adaptive) by hand: It clamps CPU to 15 W on SEN5 (17.24 t/s) and latches EC reg 0x32 = 1 until cold shutdown.
  • Do NOT install CPU-only thermald expecting clean-boot brake elimination: Without DPST(1), 34 W GPU draw alone still pushes SKIN to 59.05 °C and triggers the EC fallback.

6. Going Further: Two Paths to 25–30 t/s (If Needed Later)

Our clean-boot ceiling (~20.4 t/s at 48.4 W) reflects the XPS 15 9530's passive chassis cooling limit under Dell's conservative no-DPTF EC fallback. Sustaining 28–30 t/s physically requires ~65–68 W. Because we chose hardware longevity over running an out-of-warranty thin-and-light laptop at its thermal redline, we stopped at the safe 48.4 W preset — but two clear upgrade paths are documented and ready if ever needed:

Option 1 · Active Underside Cooling Safest → ~28–30 t/s

Pointing high-static-pressure airflow under the chassis (e.g., an IETS GT500 / Llano-style sealed-foam cooler pad, or even a desk fan aimed underneath the lifted laptop) directly cools the bottom skin (SKIN) and charger area (SEN6 / QE10).

Shaving just 4–6 °C off SKIN and SEN6 unlocks the full 68 W stock envelope (28–30 t/s sustained) with zero software hacks, EC reg 0x32 = 0 untouched, and 100% stock hardware protection.

Test with no sudo: TRACE_ROUNDS=8 bash <(sed 's/^ARMS=.*/ARMS="balanced"/' strata-throttle-trace.sh)

Option 2 · Windows-Style DPST(1) + Custom thermald Software → ~25–28 t/s

Mirrors Windows Intel DTT: perform the one-way _OSC → DPST(1) handoff (EC reg 0x32 = 1) to disable the legacy 62–65 °C EC brake, then run a custom thermald curve (59 °C → 22–26 W passive + 66 °C → 15 W max backstop) keeping SEN6 ≤ 65–66 °C (below Dell's own Windows GDDV limits of _HOT = 70 °C / _CRT = 75 °C).

Trade-off: Relies on software + an external SEN6 ≥ 68 °C kill-switch guard rather than the EC legacy brake until cold shutdown; runs the charger circuit warmer (~64–66 °C).
